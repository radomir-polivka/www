Thorens TP90 tonearm - cartridge selection

Here I share my experience with the TP90 tonearm and various cartridges.

I use the TP90 on a Thorens TD 320 mkIII turntable with Pro-Ject Phono Box S2 Ultra phono stage. I listen to 60's/70's era analogue recordings of acoustic music, mainly chamber and orchestral works from baroque and classical period, often HIP. Keep this in mind when reading the information below - music genre can have a significant impact on cartridge selection.

My experience

  • Ortofon 2M Blue: lacking in detail, lower mids and bass a little overwhelming. I tested a used cartridge, so this may have been caused by stylus wear.
  • Audio-Technica VM530EN: the very opposite of the 2M Blue. Very detailed, MC-like sound, but lacking in bass.
  • Ortofon Quintet Red: similar to the VM530EN, with a slightly better balance between treble and bass. The step to MC did not bring the expected improvement, The price difference not fully justified.
  • Audio-Technica AT33PTG/II: my final cartridge for quite some time now and the most balanced of the four. Lots of detail, sybilance under control, but still a little bass-shy.

I conclude the MicroLine stylus is what makes the AT33PTG/II superior to the others for my use. I also doubt that MC is necessary for me. This is my current assumption which may prove completely wrong, as e.g. the new AT33xEN with nude elliptical stylus sounds amazing in this comparison video.

MicroLine / Shibata cartridges survey

As the AT33PTG/II is out of production, I prepared the survey below of currently available models with an advanced stylus shape (MicroLine, Fine Line, Special Line or Shibata) that cost less than 750 EUR.

The tables also show the compatibility with the Thorens TP90 tonearm. For a medium-heavy arm (effective mass 17 g), a cartridge with low to medium compliance fits best. Except this single column, the tables should provide a general overivew of the advanced stylus cartridges available in October 2026.

Columns

  • Stylus – ML MicroLine, FL Fine Line, SL Special Line / line contact, SH Shibata, followed by the tip dimensions in µm (minor/major axis) where known; ? = major axis not published. Values published in mil were converted (1 mil = 25.4 µm), hover over them for the original.
  • Cnt. – cantilever material: Al aluminium, B boron, Du duralumin; ? = not known (yet).
  • Price – lowest retail price incl. VAT in German or Czech shops, checked Sep 2026 “+” means the price of the replacement stylus only (note 2).
  • Cmpl. – dynamic compliance at 10 Hz in µm/mN (cu). Japanese makers and Sumiko quote it at 100 Hz; those values were multiplied by 1.7 as Audio-Technica advises. * = estimate or unofficial value (notes 4, 6).
  • Wt – cartridge weight.
  • Res. – resonance frequency with the TP90 arm, calculated as f = 159 / √((17 g + cartridge weight + 2 g screws) × compliance). The arm/cartridge resonance should be around 8–12 Hz.
  • Ht – height. Ortofon heights include the stylus; confirmed by measuring a Quintet Red. Audio-Technica does not say whether its heights include the stylus, but at least for AT33PTG/II I confimed it does.
  • VTF – recommended vertical tracking force.
  • R, C – recommended load resistance and, for MM, load capacitance.

Left out

Over 750 EUR: AT33Sa, Ortofon MC X40, Quintet Black S, 2M Black LVB 250. Also left out are cartridges with elliptical, conical or spherical styli (e.g. AT-VM530, Ortofon Quintet Red and Blue, MC X10 and X20, the Nagaoka MP series).
