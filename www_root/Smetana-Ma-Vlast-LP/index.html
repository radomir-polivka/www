Bedřich Smetana: Má vlast / analogue LP recordings

Work in progress, last edit October 13, 2026.

Surprisingly, not so many recordings of complete Má vlast cycle were done in analogue era and only a few of them are satisfying. This page should give a complete list; early mono recordings as well as later digital recordings are left out. I aim to review and compare the sound character of these recordings, as this sort of information seems missing on internet and the old printed reviews are not easily available any more.

Fitting six parts on LP

The cycle consists of six parts, which are traditionally fitted on two LPs or even squeezed onto 3 sides. When two parts are places on the same side, they are up to 28 mintues long. As all parts culminate in a romatic forte finale, it is clear they cannot be cut with all due dynamics without hitting distortion in inner grooves. The cycle would deserve 3 LPs.

Rafael Kubelík

Vienna Philharmonic Orchestra, rec. 1958, Decca

This has been released many times since 1958 and a modern reissue by Speakers Corner exists as well. Analogue Anorak reports:

  • The early Ace of Diamonds stereo pressing (mid 1960s, grooved label version) has a brittle string sound.
  • The mono re-press from the same period sounds better.
  • The mid 1970s budget release, cut with an improved head, has a better string sound, but is still not perfect.
  • All of the above was questioned by other reviewers.

Based on Decca 1992 digital release, I confirm the sound is not exceptional and for me it does not make sense to invest to the Speaker's Corner LP reissue. Musical qualities are indisputable though.

Boston Symphony Orchestra, rec. 1971, Deutsche Grammophon

The original DG release has narrow, nasal sound, especially in the strings.

Analogue Anorak reports that the Original Source Series reissue sounds good overall but still mentiones severe issues, mainly inner groove distortion on sides 2 and 4. These sides are cut to the very limit, prioritizing dynamics at the expense of the inner groove problems. This is very opposite of how Berglund EMI recording is cut.

Karel Ančerl, Czech Philharmonic Orchestra, rec. 1963, Supraphon

Subpar string sound, similar to that of Kubelík's 1971 Deutsche Grammophon recording, but a little more bearable.

Václav Neumann, Gewandhaus Orchestra Leipzig, rec. 1968, Eterna

I have not heard the LP yet; the notes below are based on the DSD release:

  • slower tempos, more pathetic spirit
  • more direct sound, better instrument separation, wider stereo image and a better balanced frequency spectrum (the recordings with CPO are more nasal and mid-heavy)

Discogs release page

Václav Neumann, Czech Philharmonic Orchestra, rec. 1972, Panton

Originally a TV recording and that's exactly how it sounds like. No dynamics, direct dry sound, prominent and unnatural mids, not enough bass extension. Tympani sound like .. on TV. The sound is the very opposite of being broad or mellow. The recording was probably never planned to be released as a commercial LP. Panton used the recording for a gift LP in 1981 and later it was released for normal distribution. At that time the 1975 Supraphon recording was already available and offered better sound.

Discogs release page

Václav Neumann, Czech Philharmonic Orchestra, rec. 1975, Supraphon

The last analogue recording by this orchestra. In general, it is similar to the 1972 recording (the same conductor, orchestra and hall; different recording crew). It has broader sound, more dynamics, more bass, more ambience and space, however mids sound still unnatural in some passages and the recording lacks any sonic sparkle.

International recordings

Malcolm Sargent, Royal Philharmonic Orchestra, rec. 1964, HMV

Released in 1965 as two independent LPs. Má vlast squeezed onto 3 sides. Only the first LP re-released later. I have not heard it yet, it wouldn't be easy to get a perfect copy given it is from 1965.

Discogs release page

Walter Süsskind, Saint Louis Symphony Orchestra, rec. 1975, Turnabout

Dave Hurwitz reports that the later digital release of this recording is great. It mentions the original vinyl did not sound so good, it is not clear if it was due to his equipment at that time or due to the actual LP quality. Only quadrophonic release on LP exist.

Discogs release page

Paavo Berglund, Staatskapelle Dresden, rec. 1979, EMI

Má vlast is fitted on three sides up to 28 minutes long. Interestingly, the cutting engineer decided to back up from the inner area and not to cut behind the 68 mm diameter. No inner groove distortion thanks to that, but the recording lacks the wild dynamics typical for classical Decca or RCA recordings. Other than this, the sound is natural and broad. The sound color is better that that of 1975 Neumann/CPO recording while the dynamics seems more compressed than on Supraphon. Note that some later releases on LP are digitally remastered. Dave Hurwitz gives the digital release of this recording praise.

And..

Jakub Hrůša, Bamberg Symphony, rec. 2020

A direct-cut release on three LPs at 45 rpm, the holy grail. It was a limited edition and is no longer available at a reasonable price.

Left out

There is a notable Talich mono recording and one more Kubelik mono recording. There are many exceptional digital recordings, among them the Harnoncourt's or one more Kubelík's with CPO from 1990.
